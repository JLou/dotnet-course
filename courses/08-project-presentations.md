# Session 8 — Pair-project presentations

**Duration:** 180 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Demonstrate a working project outcome and explain its architecture.
- Relate implementation choices to course topics such as testing, ASP.NET Core, EF Core, dependency injection, workers, Aspire, or AOT.
- Give and receive concise technical feedback.
- Reflect on trade-offs and what they would improve with additional time.

## Session outline

| Time | Activity |
|---:|---|
| 10 min | Setup and presentation guidance |
| 10 min | Break |
| 150 min | Pair-project showcase: 10 minutes per pair |
| 10 min | Course wrap-up and reflection |
| **180 min** | **Total** |

## Presentation format

Each pair gets **10 minutes**:

- **8 minutes:** Explain the problem and demonstrate the project.
- **2 minutes:** Answer questions.

At 10 minutes per pair, the 150-minute showcase allows up to 15 pairs. For larger cohorts, arrange a second presentation block, simultaneous poster/demo sessions, or longer total presentation time outside this three-hour meeting.

## Guidance to give students

Ask each pair to cover:

1. The user/problem and the scope they chose.
2. A short live demonstration of the main end-to-end feature.
3. A diagram or concise explanation of the main components and data flow.
4. How they used tests and what behavior those tests protect.
5. One technical trade-off or challenge they encountered.
6. What they would improve next.

Encourage students to show working behavior rather than spend most of the slot on slides. They should not try to mention every course technology; explain the choices they actually made.

## Instructor preparation

- Publish the presentation order and time limits before the session.
- Prepare a visible timer and a consistent question format.
- Ask teams to verify their demo from a clean startup and prepare a fallback in case a live service fails.
- Use a rubric aligned with course outcomes. Possible dimensions include working functionality, technical explanation, testing/quality, and presentation clarity; adapt weighting to the course's assessment rules.
- If this presentation is formally graded, communicate rubric and individual-contribution expectations in advance.

## Wrap-up — 10 min

- Summarize the course path from SDK/tooling through application frameworks and deployment/runtime choices.
- Ask students to name one concept they can now apply and one area they would investigate further.
- Remind students to ensure their repository and project documentation are in the agreed final state.
